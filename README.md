# StagRAG
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is increasingly applied in medical and healthcare settings to ground large language models in biomedical evidence and reduce hallucinations. However, most existing RAG hallucination detection methods are directly adapted from LLM output monitoring, which only flags whether an answer is reliable, but cannot reveal where in the pipeline the failure originates. This is particularly problematic in healthcare, where errors may come from missing retrieval, suboptimal reranking, or unstable generation, and the inability to localize failure sources undermines both accuracy and transparency. To address this gap, we propose a **Stag**e-wise **RAG** diagnostic framework (StagRAG) that attributes errors to the retriever ($R$), reranker ($Re$), or generator ($G$). By introducing oracle-guided interventions and auxiliary inference probes, our method goes beyond simple hallucination detection and provides interpretable explanations of error origins. Empirical evaluation shows that many failures in medical RAG tasks stem from insufficient retrieval coverage and generator instability. Our work highlights the importance of system-level transparency in medical AI, offering a first step toward building trustworthy, error-aware RAG systems that can support clinical decision-making and patient-facing applications.
